# Version 2 plan

Written 14 November 2026, after an audit of version 1.0.0.

Status marks used below: **OK** = present and adequate. **THIN** = present but too shallow to act on. **MISSING** = not in the manual.

---

## 1. Audit of version 1

Version 1 has the right skeleton: a measurable definition of the goal, evidence ratings, tiered standards, a scorecard and a review rhythm. Its weaknesses are below, most serious first.

### It tells you what the standard is, but often not how to get there
- Body is the only domain with a staged protocol. Mind, Presence, People and Character are mostly lists of drills with no progression.
- Nutrition is four lines. Sleep is one standard with no method. There is nothing on mobility, posture, injury prevention or preventive health checks.
- Money gives an order of operations but no worked example, no allocation logic and nothing on tax, credit, insurance sizing or large purchases.

### Self-knowledge is one subjective slider
- The scorecard is a 1 to 10 self-rating. There are no objective baseline tests, so the first snapshot is a guess and later ones inherit the bias.
- No personality, strengths, time or money audit. The system does not know where your hours and rupees go today.

### The evidence is uneven
- Body and Principles are cited. Presence, Character and most of Mind have no sources at all.
- Popular claims that do not hold up are only covered in the Principles section. Each domain has its own myths (supplements, productivity, charisma, dating, investing) and none are addressed.
- Seven of 21 sources are summaries rather than the original paper.

### Whole areas of a man's life are absent
- Practical competence: cooking, driving, swimming, first aid, basic repairs, self-defence, etiquette, travel.
- Environment and admin: where you live, documents, digital security, routines for moving city.
- Vices and compulsions: alcohol, nicotine, gambling, compulsive screen and porn use.
- Leadership and management, which the roadmap assumes from year 4 but nothing teaches.
- Play, rest and enjoyment. The failure section warns about joylessness and then offers nothing for it.
- Family responsibilities beyond a weekly call.

### The execution layer is thin
- No ideal week that shows how seven domains fit into 168 hours.
- No 12-week sprint page. "One focus per quarter" has no place to plan or track it.
- No handling of seasons: exam weeks, joining a job, relocation, travel, illness, festivals.
- No risk register, no decision frameworks, no accountability mechanism.
- Standards have no verification rule and no date-achieved record, so progress over years is invisible.

### The roadmap is generic
- Phases have themes but no exit criteria.
- It is not tied to real dates: end of the MBA, joining date, first appraisal.

### The page itself
- 83 KB in one scroll with no search, no per-section progress and no trend view of snapshots.
- Account saving has not been confirmed in live use.

## 2. Design rules for version 2

1. **Every domain has the same ten-part anatomy** (section 4). A reader should always know where to look.
2. **Standards become verifiable.** Each one states how it is checked and records the date it was first met.
3. **Three depths.** A one-page summary per domain, the full protocol, and a reference layer. The summary must be enough to run the quarter.
4. **Evidence or label.** Every factual claim is cited and logged, or marked as judgement. Each domain gets a myth file.
5. **Completeness without overload.** The manual holds everything. The quarter still runs on one focus. The page must make that separation obvious.
6. **Personal where it matters.** Baselines, dates and constraints are yours. Generic advice is the fallback only.
7. **No manipulation content.** Presence and People teach skill and honesty. Tactics designed to deceive or pressure people stay out.

## 4. The ten-part domain anatomy

Every domain in Part C contains, in this order:

1. **One-page summary.** The standard in one line, the three actions that matter most, the one metric to watch.
2. **Standards.** Floor, Strong, Elite. Each with a verification rule and a date-met field.
3. **Baseline test.** How to measure where you are today.
4. **Evidence.** What research supports, with ratings.
5. **Protocol by stage.** Beginner, intermediate, advanced. What to do each week.
6. **Playbooks.** Step-by-step guides for the specific situations in that domain.
7. **Metrics.** Lead measures (what you do weekly) and lag measures (what changes over months).
8. **Workbook.** Fill-in pages.
9. **Failure modes and myths.** How it goes wrong and what popular advice to ignore.
10. **Resources.** Books, courses, tools.

## 5. Research agenda

Claims that need sourcing before they go in. Primary papers, meta-analyses and consensus statements only.

**Body.** Sleep regularity and health. Sleep hygiene components that work. Protein distribution and plant versus animal protein. Vitamin D and B12 in Indian adults. Resistance training frequency. Interval training and VO2 max. Stretching and injury. Alcohol and health. Caffeine timing. Preventive screening guidance for men in their twenties and thirties.

**Mind.** CBT efficacy. Mindfulness meditation effects and their size. Exercise and depression. Expressive writing. Smartphone presence and attention, including failed replications. Multitasking costs. Exposure and anxiety. Decision journals and forecasting calibration.

**Craft.** Conscientiousness and job performance. Predictors of early-career pay growth. Feedback-seeking. Sponsorship versus mentorship. Negotiation outcomes. Job-hopping and pay. Management practices and performance.

**Money.** Index funds versus active funds in India (SPIVA India). Savings rate arithmetic. Behaviour gap in investor returns. Lifestyle inflation.

**Presence.** First impressions research. Power posing replication failure. Filler words and credibility. Voice pitch and pace. Dress and perception. Public speaking anxiety interventions.

**People.** Friendship formation hours. Loneliness and health. Gottman's predictors of relationship breakdown and their critique. Attachment theory in adults. Weak ties and job outcomes. Active-constructive responding.

**Character.** Honesty interventions. Self-control and life outcomes (Dunedin study). Gratitude and prosocial behaviour effects. Meaning in life and health.

**Myth file candidates.** Power posing. 10,000 hours. Learning styles. Dopamine detox. Cold exposure for testosterone. Testosterone "optimisation" supplements. Alpha and beta hierarchy. Semen-retention claims. 5 a.m. routines. Multitasking. Body-language "93% rule".
