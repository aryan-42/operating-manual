The ground the rest stands on: where you live, your papers, your digital security, your tools, and the rest and play that keep you human.

One-page summary

The standard
Nothing important can be lost to one stolen phone, one missed renewal or one bad month. Your room helps you do the right thing. You rest properly and do some things only because you enjoy them.
How it runs
One setup weekend, then one admin hour a month and one audit a year. Like Competence, it is rarely the quarter's focus.

What the evidence says

  • A second step stops most account takeovers. In Google's year-long study, a prompt on the user's own phone blocked 100% of automated attacks, 99% of bulk phishing and 90% of targeted attacks. Codes sent by SMS did worse against targeted attacks, and physical security keys did best.55
  • Switching off from work is recovery. A meta-analysis of recovery research found that people who mentally detach from work in their off hours report less fatigue.56 An evening spent half-working is neither work nor rest.

Protocol

SetupOne weekend
  • Saturday morning, digital: password manager, new passwords on the five accounts that matter, two-step sign-in, SIM lock, backups on.
  • Saturday afternoon, papers: gather, scan, name, store in two places, fill in the document list.
  • Sunday morning, space: clear the room, set up the desk and the training corner, throw out what you do not use.
  • Sunday afternoon, calendar: the monthly admin hour, the weekly day off and the annual audit entered as repeating events.
MonthlyOne hour
  • Bills, subscriptions and bank statements checked line by line.
  • Anything expiring in the next 90 days renewed or booked.
  • Photos and files from the phone backed up and the inbox cleared.
  • One thing fixed that has been broken for more than a month.
YearlyWith the annual review
  • Digital audit: close unused accounts, review app permissions and logged-in devices, change any reused password.
  • Nominees and the "if something happens" page updated.
  • Document list checked against the originals.
  • Adventure list reread, and one item given a date.

Playbooks

Documents and admin one folder, two copies, a list
  1. Gather the originals into one physical folder: identity papers, passport, driving licence, degree certificates and mark sheets, offer and relieving letters, payslips and tax statements, bank and investment records, insurance policies, rent agreement, medical records, vehicle papers.
  2. Scan each one as a PDF with a clear name: type, your name, year.
  3. Store in two places: an encrypted cloud folder and an offline drive. DigiLocker holds government-issued copies of many Indian documents and is worth setting up.
  4. Fill in the document list in the workbook, with expiry and renewal dates. Put the dates in your calendar 90 days ahead.
  5. Set a nominee on every bank account, policy, provident fund and investment. It takes minutes and saves your family months.
  6. Write the "if something happens" page: who to call, where the folder is, which institutions hold your money and cover, and who has the password manager's emergency access. Give it to one person you trust.

Do not type passwords, PINs, card numbers, account numbers or identity numbers into this manual or into the page above. List where things are, not the secrets themselves.

Digital security an afternoon that removes most of the risk
  1. Password manager. One long passphrase you memorise. Every other password generated and unique.
  2. Start with mail. Whoever controls your mail can reset everything else. Longest password, strongest second step.
  3. Second step everywhere it is offered. Prefer a passkey, an authenticator app or a prompt on your phone. Use SMS only where nothing else is available.55 Save the backup codes in the password manager.
  4. Lock the SIM with a PIN, since your number receives the codes.
  5. Devices: screen lock, automatic updates, disk encryption, remote-wipe switched on.
  6. Backups: automatic, to the cloud and to a drive. Test by restoring a file.
  7. Payments. You enter a UPI PIN only to send money, never to receive it. Never share a one-time code or install a screen-sharing app because a caller asks. Keep a low daily limit on the account linked to your phone.
  8. Links and calls. Do not sign in through a link in a message. Open the app or type the address. Banks, police and courier companies do not ask for codes, and nobody is "arrested" over a video call.
  9. What you post. No documents, boarding passes, home address or live location. Assume an employer will read all of it.

If money has been taken, act within the hour: call your bank to block the account or card, then report it on the national cybercrime helpline, 1930, or at cybercrime.gov.in. Speed is what gets money frozen.

Steps other than the second-step finding are standard security practice.

Your room arranged so the right thing is the easy thing
  • Make the good action visible. Dumbbells and mat where you can see them. The book on the pillow. A water bottle on the desk.
  • Make the bad action awkward. Phone charger outside the bedroom. No food at the desk. The television, if there is one, not facing the bed.
  • One clear surface for deep work, used for nothing else.
  • A place for everything you use daily. Keys, wallet, bag, shoes. You should be able to leave in two minutes.
  • Spend on what you touch for hours: mattress, pillow, chair, shoes. Save on the rest.
  • Own less. Each term, remove what you have not used.
  • The weekly reset is in the Competence home playbook. Do it the same day every week.

Tools and systems four, and no more
  • One calendar. Everything with a time goes in it, including training and the people slots.
  • One task list. Everything you have agreed to do. Emptied into the week at the weekly review.
  • One notes home. Your commonplace book, for what you read and think.
  • This manual. For standards, scores and reviews.

Each new app is something else to maintain. Add one only when a real problem has stayed unsolved for a month, and remove one when you do.

Rest, play and adventure the part ambitious people cut first
  • A weekly day off. No work, no coursework, no manual. Guard it as you guard training.
  • An evening boundary. A fixed time after which work is closed. Detaching is what makes the rest count.56
  • One thing with no payoff. A sport, an instrument, quizzing, cooking, a game with friends. If you catch yourself trying to make it productive, pick another.
  • Leave the city a few times a year. One night away resets more than a week of evenings.
  • Keep an adventure list. Twenty things you want to have done: a trek, a long train route, a country, a skill, a race. Date one each year.
  • Take your leave. All of it.

The check from "When it breaks" belongs here: did I enjoy anything this quarter that had no payoff? If not, that is the constraint to fix next.

How this domain fails, and what to ignore

  • Treating admin as something to do "when free". It is done in the monthly hour or it is done in a crisis.
  • Rebuilding your tools in place of using them. A new notes app is not progress.
  • Rest that is half work. It produces neither.
  • One password, reused. It fails all at once.
  • Ignore: productivity setups with a dozen apps, minimalism as an identity, and anyone who says rest must be earned.
