00Myth file

Popular self-improvement claims that did not survive testing. Most "high performance" content is built on at least one of these.

A man chasing the top 1% is the target customer for every one of these ideas. Knowing which ones failed saves years. Each row gives the claim, what happened when it was tested properly, and what to do with the time instead.

Not yet sourced

These are widely repeated. The verdicts below are judgement and have not yet been tied to a specific study in this manual. They are on the list for the next research sweep.

  • Dopamine detox. The name misdescribes how dopamine works. What helps is the ordinary part: fewer compulsive inputs.
  • Waking at 5 a.m. A regular wake time matters. The hour does not. Sleeping less to wake earlier is a net loss.
  • Cold showers, semen retention and "testosterone-boosting" supplements. No convincing evidence that any of these changes hormones or performance in healthy young men. Sleep, training, body fat and alcohol are what move the needle.
  • Alpha and beta males. Borrowed from early wolf research that its own author later corrected. It is a poor model of human status, which rests on competence and generosity as much as dominance.
  • "93% of communication is body language." A misreading of two small 1960s experiments about conflicting signals. Words carry most of the content in almost every real exchange.
  • Multitasking. Switching between tasks costs time and accuracy on both. Single-task blocks are a core part of Mind.

The filter for anything new

Before adopting any technique, ask three questions. Was it tested on more than a few dozen people? Did anyone outside the original team repeat it? Would it change what I do this week? If you cannot answer yes to all three, park it in the research inbox in the repository.
