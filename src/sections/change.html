00How change works

The mechanics of changing behaviour over years, so that the dip in week four does not surprise you.

Lead measures and lag measures

A lag measure is the result you want: waist size, a promotion, net worth. It moves slowly and you cannot act on it directly. A lead measure is the action that produces it: training sessions done, deep-work hours, rupees invested. You can hit a lead measure this week.

Every domain in this manual lists both. Plan and review on lead measures. Check lag measures monthly or quarterly. Judging yourself weekly on a lag measure is the fastest way to lose heart, because it barely moves in a week.

The shape of a new habit

Week 1 to 2Novelty carries you. It feels easy and you want to add more.Do not add. Do less than you could and never miss.

The timings follow the habit research: early repetitions add the most automaticity, the median plateau is around day 66, and complex habits such as exercise take longer.8 The week labels are a guide, not a finding.

Why plans fail

  • Too many changes at once. Each new behaviour competes for the same attention.
  • Outcome goals with no action attached. "Get fit" has no Tuesday.
  • No fixed cue. "When I have time" never arrives.
  • All or nothing. One miss is read as failure and the whole plan is dropped.
  • Unchanged surroundings. The same room, phone and schedule produce the same behaviour.
  • Borrowed goals. A goal you do not own gets abandoned the first time it costs something.

The four moves this manual uses

  1. One focus. A single domain per 12-week sprint, with two or three lead measures.
  2. Fixed cue. Every action has a day, a time and a place, written as an if-then plan.
  3. A floor. A version so small it survives your worst day: the minimum viable day in Rhythm.
  4. Evidence of progress. Ticked standards with dates, retested baselines, quarterly snapshots. Belief in yourself is built from that record.17
